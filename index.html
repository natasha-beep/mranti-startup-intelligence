How this complements MRANTI

MYStartup can answer “what does Malaysia's startup ecosystem look like?” — active startups, geographic distribution, technology/industry mix, talent and investment signals. DOSM can answer “what is happening in the market around them?” — sector revenue, e-commerce activity and household purchasing power. MRANTI internal data then answers “what did our own portfolio commercialise?” .

Malaysia formal wage distribution

A source-clean version of the income pyramid. The bands are defined by published DOSM percentile anchors: bottom 10%, national median (50th percentile) and top 10%. We deliberately do not show estimated T20/T5/T1 thresholds as official statistics.

Median ≠ average RM3,027 is the median: half of formal employees are below this point and half are above it.
RM1,700 is also the statutory minimum wage The minimum wage is an employment-policy reference. DOSM separately reports that 7.9% of Malaysian formal employees earned below RM1,700 in March 2026.
Not Malaysia's full 34m+ population This distribution covers Malaysian formal employees, not children, students, retirees, unemployed persons, own-account workers or all informal workers.
No estimated T20 / T5 / T1 labels Those can be added only when we have exact percentile thresholds from a traceable source. Until then, the dashboard keeps published and estimated figures separate.
Source: DOSM · Employee Wages Statistics (Formal Sector), First Quarter 2026 · reference month: March 2026 · released 29 Jul 2026

Methodology: The pyramid is a visual grouping of DOSM percentile anchors, not an occupational classification. Job titles such as “CEO”, “manager”, “engineer” or “service worker” are therefore not assigned to a wage tier unless supported by a separate occupation-level wage dataset.
